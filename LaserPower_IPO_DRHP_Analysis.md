**Subscription view (retrospective — stock already listed):** **WATCH-POST-LISTING.** No RED-level fraud/governance flag, but several YELLOW flags (debt-heavy fresh issue, promoter OFS, an unresolved criminal complaint against promoters tied to an RPT counterparty, repeated auditor emphasis-of-matter, high working-capital intensity). ## 1. Business Overview (p.230)

Kolkata-based (est. 1988) manufacturer of power cables, conductors and specialty products, plus an EPC arm (entered 2015) executing rural electrification, distribution infrastructure, substations and turnkey transmission projects for state discoms. Manufacturing units in Howrah and Kharagpur (West Bengal); sales footprint across East and Northeast India (Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Mizoram, Meghalaya, Manipur, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura per p.147 basis-for-offer-price note). Company came into its current form via a 2025 composite scheme of arrangement (demerger from Lumino Industries Ltd + amalgamation of ~14 group entities) — a corporate restructuring worth independently verifying didn't just repackage related-party assets ahead of listing.

## 2. Industry & Market (p.163)

Positions itself as "one of the leading players in manufacturing capacity for power cables and conductors in East India" — a regional, not national, claim. Industry section relies on a paid consultant report (standard practice, but market-size/share claims should be treated as issuer-favorable, not independently verified).

## 3. Objects of the Offer (p.137)

- Fresh Issue: ₹5,420mn (₹542 cr) | OFS: ₹2,000mn (₹200 cr) | **Total offer: ₹7,420mn (₹742 cr)**, at ₹214/share.
- Net proceeds from fresh issue: ₹5,111.46mn. Of this, **₹4,900mn (96% of net proceeds) goes to debt repayment**; only ₹211.46mn (4%) to general corporate purposes (capped at 25% of gross proceeds by SEBI rule).
- **No capex/growth funding object at all.** This is a deleveraging IPO, not a growth-capital raise — worth flagging clearly since "IPO to fund expansion" is the more common (and more bullish) narrative.
- OFS entirely by three promoters: Deepak Goel (₹112.5cr), Devesh Goel (₹62.5cr), Rakhi Goel (₹25cr) — cost of acquisition ₹0.01–₹0.10/share, i.e. near-total capital gain. OFS is ~27% of total issue size — below the ">50% = promoters cashing out" threshold but still a material insider sell alongside a debt-paydown-only fresh issue.

**Flag: FY26 operating cash flow is negative (-₹119cr) despite ₹152cr net profit** — a classic profit/cash divergence. Debtor days spiked back up to 216 in FY26 (from 159 in FY25) and the balance sheet shows "Other Assets" (largely receivables/WIP) growing from ₹2,054cr to ₹2,389cr. Screener's own auto-flagged "Con" is exactly this: **"Company has high debtors of 216 days."** Borrowings also jumped from ₹504cr to ₹871cr in FY26 — funding the working-capital gap with debt, which is precisely what part of the IPO proceeds are now being used to pay down. This combination (profit up, cash down, debt up, then IPO to repay that debt) is the single most important number in this filing and deserves more scrutiny than the qualitative "growth story."

## 6. Risk Factors (p.22) — most specific, non-boilerplate ones

- **Auditor emphasis of matter for 3 consecutive years (FY24–FY26)** covering: outstanding title deeds for immovable properties, discrepancies between working-capital statements filed with banks vs. books, loans/guarantees to related and other parties, and disputed statutory dues (p.425, MD&A "Reservations, Qualifications and Adverse Remarks"). Not a qualified opinion, but a repeat emphasis-of-matter for three straight years is a governance signal worth more weight than a one-off.
- Customer/counterparty blacklisting history: company has been in litigation over **being blacklisted or threatened with blacklisting by two state power distribution utilities** (Manipur SPDCL, UP's erstwhile discoms) and a rejected-technical-bid dispute with a UP RDSS contractor — see §6 below.
- Long EPC execution cycles (6–48 months) with cost/time overrun risk typical of the sector.
- Late-delivery deductions from customers were ₹17.4mn/₹7.9mn/₹3.5mn in FY24/25/26 — shrinking, a mild positive.

## Addendum 2: EPS accretion from debt paydown, growth guidance, true competitors, anchor investors

### Is the debt reduction EPS-accretive?

Short answer: **roughly EPS-neutral, not a real accretion driver** — the interest saved on the ₹490cr debt repayment almost exactly offsets the dilution from the 25.3mn new shares issued in the fresh issue. It's a balance-sheet story (deleveraging, lower Net Debt/EBITDA), not an EPS-growth story.

The math (all figures from the Prospectus, p.130 pre/post-Offer share count, p.137 net proceeds, p.431 borrowing rates, screener.in FY26 PAT/tax rate):

- FY26 PAT (pre-transaction): ₹152cr on 115.04mn pre-Offer shares → EPS ₹13.21 (ties to the Prospectus's own disclosed ₹13.18, small rounding diff, good cross-check).
- Post-Offer share count: 140.37mn (+22% dilution from the 25.33mn fresh-issue shares).
- **Dilution alone** (before any interest saving): EPS falls to ₹10.83 (-18%).
- Debt being repaid: ₹490cr, drawn from a mix disclosed at p.431 — secured facilities at 7.15–9.75% p.a., unsecured ICD at a fixed 12% p.a. A blended ~8.5–9% is a reasonable estimate given the repayment is likely to prioritize the highest-cost unsecured/ICD tranches.
- Post-tax interest saved at a ~9% blended rate, ~22% effective tax rate (per screener/Prospectus tax line): **≈ ₹34cr added to PAT**.
- **Net effect: adjusted PAT ≈ ₹186cr, adjusted EPS ≈ ₹13.2–13.5** on the enlarged 140.37mn share base — i.e., **within ~1-2% of the pre-Offer EPS of ₹13.21**, across the disclosed 7.15%–9.75%/12% rate range.

So: dilution (-18%) and interest savings (+16–18%) roughly cancel out. This is a materially different, more sober framing than the "IPO deleverages the balance sheet" narrative Choice Broking used approvingly (Addendum 1) — deleveraging is real and helps future borrowing capacity/rating, but investors shouldn't expect an EPS pop from the debt paydown itself. Any EPS growth from here has to come from operating profit growth, not financial engineering.

*(Caveat: I don't have the exact facility-by-facility repayment allocation — the Prospectus says "certain outstanding borrowings," not which specific loans — so the blended rate is an estimate within the disclosed 7.15–12% band, not a precise figure.)*

### Growth guidance (qualitative — no numerical revenue/EBITDA targets are given; SEBI prohibits forward-looking financial projections in Indian prospectuses)

From "Our Business – Strategies" (p.241–244), the five stated pillars:

1. **Reconductoring/HTLS upgrade cycle** — bid pipeline of 18 projects worth ~₹900cr specifically in HTLS reconductoring; company frames the shift from conventional ACSR to HTLS/AECC as a structural, multi-year replacement cycle (CRISIL-sourced, so treat directionally).
2. **Diversify into water distribution, solar EPC, BESS** — very early stage: **one water project and one solar EPC project executed to date** despite entering these segments in 2018 (solar) and 2022 (water). This is aspirational language well ahead of actual traction — the "diversification" strategy has almost no track record yet.
3. **Automation/R&D** — MES + ERP + Power BI MIS dashboards already implemented; in-house R&D lab at Unit I. Real but incremental, cost/quality-focused rather than a new growth vector.
4. **Domestic + export geographic expansion** — existing exports to Ghana, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Mauritius, Mozambique, Nepal, Nigeria, Afghanistan, Ethiopia, Kuwait, Rwanda, UAE, plus one EPC project in Togo — a wide but thin geographic footprint (single projects in many of these markets, not a scaled export franchise).
5. **Deepen Eastern India stronghold** while selectively expanding pan-India via HTLS/MVCC technology.
