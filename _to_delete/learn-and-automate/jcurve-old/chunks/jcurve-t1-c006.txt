1. Smart Money Accumulates Quietly When institutions want to build a position, they do it gradually to avoid driving up prices too quickly. This accumulation phase often happens when the stock is out of favor, underperforming, or in a consolidation range. You’ll see relatively low trading volumes with periodic spikes. 2. Stock Rallies & Retail Interest Grows As the stock starts moving up, media coverage, social media hype, and positive earnings reports attract retail investors. By this time, institutions have already built a solid position. Price action looks strong, and more people jump in, thinking the rally has just begun. 3. Smart Money Distributes Holdings Once retail participation increases, institutions begin offloading their positions. This distribution happens gradually so that it doesn’t trigger panic selling. You might see high volume on up days, but the stock struggles to make new highs. 4. Price Rolls Over, Leaving Retail Holding the Bag After exiting, the stock suddenly faces selling pressure. Without institutional support, it struggles to sustain higher prices. Retail traders, seeing losses, either panic sell or hold on, hoping for a recovery that may never come. How to Avoid This Trap? Look at Volume Trends: If a stock has been rising with declining volume, it might indicate a distribution phase. Track Institutional Activity: Bulk/block deals, FII/DII holdings, and insider selling are red flags. Watch for Failed Breakouts: If a stock repeatedly fails to sustain new highs, smart money could be exiting. Post-Earnings Moves: If a stock reacts poorly despite good results, institutions might be selling into strength.
