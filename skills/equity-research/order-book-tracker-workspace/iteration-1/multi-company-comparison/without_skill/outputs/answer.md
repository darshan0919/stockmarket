# RVNL vs NCC vs Kirloskar Oil Engines — order book and revenue visibility

**As of 31 July 2026. All three companies' baselines come from the same quarter (Q4 FY26 concall, 202603), so the comparison is like-for-like on timing.**

## The answer

**RVNL has the strongest revenue visibility of the three**, but the margin over NCC is narrower than the headline ratio suggests, and Kirloskar Oil Engines shouldn't really be in this comparison at all — it doesn't run an order-book business.

## Why RVNL edges it

Three things separate RVNL, and only the first is the ratio.

**Backlog depth.** At 5.15x FY26 revenue, RVNL has roughly five years of work booked versus NCC's four. In absolute terms it is ₹18,000 Cr more work on a slightly smaller revenue base.

**A dated execution tail, not just a number.** Six of RVNL's seven post-concall wins carry a stated execution period in the filing, and they extend the visible work-in-hand out to **December 2029**. NCC discloses order intake as a monthly aggregate letter with no execution period at all, so its ₹87,079 Cr has no dated runway attached to it in the filings — you have to take management's "~4x, multi-year" characterisation on trust. When two companies both claim multi-year visibility, the one whose filings actually state the periods is the more verifiable claim.

**Intake momentum, and this is the striking one.** RVNL booked ₹5,823 Cr in the four months since the concall. Management's own disclosure at that concall was that full-year FY26 standalone order inflows were **₹5,875 Cr**. So RVNL has matched an entire year of prior-year intake in four months. Annualised, its intake is running at 0.87x revenue versus NCC's 0.78x, meaning RVNL is replacing its backlog faster than it burns it while NCC is very slightly not.

**Management is also willing to underwrite it.** RVNL guided to 15–20% revenue growth for FY26–27 and projects bidding-works execution of ₹6,283 Cr, up 129% year on year. NCC's management explicitly **withdrew FY27 revenue, margin, and inflow guidance**, deferring for a quarter citing environmental uncertainty and client funding risk. A record backlog that management won't convert into a number is weaker visibility than a smaller backlog that they will.

## Why NCC is closer than the ratio implies

NCC's book is arguably the better-quality one per rupee, and this is the honest counterargument:

- **74% of the order book is covered by escalation clauses.** RVNL's protection comes from pass-through on the nomination portion only.
- **Seven verticals, none dominant** — Buildings ₹22,740 Cr, Transportation ₹15,320 Cr, Electrical ₹13,800 Cr, Water/Railways ₹10,400 Cr, Mining ₹6,750 Cr, Irrigation ₹4,950 Cr. RVNL is **57% Railways** (₹57,000 Cr of ₹99,262 Cr), with signalling another ₹14,900 Cr on top. That is heavy dependence on one client, the Ministry of Railways, and that client already caused a **₹3,400 Cr receivables delay** that only cleared in April 2026. Concentration risk of that kind is exactly the thing that turns a backlog into an unrealised backlog.
- **Management confirmed the entire backlog is execution-ready** with clearances secured and no client-mandated delays — a specific claim RVNL did not make.
- NCC's backlog is at a record high, up 16% year on year, achieved while revenue _fell_ 6%.

The counterweight: NCC's FY26 standalone revenue declined 9% and consolidated declined 6%, so a rising book against falling execution means the book is growing partly because work isn't converting. Visibility of revenue you can't execute is worth less. RVNL's revenue was roughly flat (₹19,923 → ₹20,412 Cr).

Net: **RVNL on quantum, duration and momentum; NCC on quality and durability.** If the question were "whose backlog is least likely to disappoint", NCC would be defensible. On visibility as asked — how much revenue is contracted and for how long — RVNL wins.

## Kirloskar Oil Engines is a category mismatch

KIRLOSENG's 0.10x is not a finding about weak visibility; it is a disclosure artefact. The ₹798 Cr it reports is the combined NPCIL and Marine specialised order book — two long-cycle contracts inside a business that is otherwise short-cycle gensets, industrial engines and B2C products sold off the shelf. There is no company-wide backlog to compare, because that business model doesn't generate one.

Two corroborating points:

- **It filed zero Reg-30 order-win announcements** since the Q4 FY26 concall — 51 filings scanned, none of them order awards. An EPC company of any size cannot go four months without an order filing; a product company routinely does.
- On the ₹798 Cr it does report, management confirmed that **quarterly billing and revenue recognition on the NPCIL contract have not yet started**, so even that sliver isn't converting yet.

Kirloskar's forward visibility rests on demand-side arguments instead: FY26 revenue up 22% to ₹7,701 Cr, powergen up 32% for the year, a data-centre push targeted at a double-digit share of powergen revenue, and a stated ₹5,000–6,000 Cr peak revenue potential from new capacity. That may well be a better business — the market thinks so, the stock is up 141% over a year while RVNL and NCC are both down ~35% — but it is a _growth_ case, not a _visibility_ case. Judged on contracted revenue in hand, it ranks last, and the ranking is close to meaningless.

## What could make this wrong

- **The running totals drift high.** Each base is net of work already executed, but the post-concall additions are gross. Between concalls, both RVNL's ₹1,05,085 Cr and NCC's ₹87,079 Cr overstate the true outstanding book by roughly one quarter of execution — on the order of ₹5,000 Cr each. This affects both similarly, so the _ranking_ is safe; the absolute figures are not precise. The Q1 FY27 concall will reset them.
- **The two intake windows are not the same length.** NCC discloses monthly and its July letter isn't filed yet, so its intake is visible only through 30 June. RVNL's runs through 28 July. The totals are annualised separately to compensate, but NCC's ₹4,075 Cr will grow by roughly a month's worth when the July letter lands, likely narrowing the intake gap.
- **NCC's June figure (₹534.85 Cr) was read at medium confidence**; the other nine order values across both companies were high-confidence, cross-checked against the SEBI word-form amount in the filing.
- **Execution end-dates are derived, not stated.** Filings give a duration ("1095 Days", "42 months"); the window is anchored on the filing date. December 2029 is therefore an inference, not a date RVNL published.
- **Book-to-bill uses FY26 revenue as the denominator.** If RVNL delivers its guided 15–20% growth, its ratio compresses to roughly 4.4x on FY27 revenue — still ahead of NCC, but the gap halves.
- One quarter's concall figure per company underpins each base. If Stockscans' synthesis of a concall mis-stated a number, this inherits it; nothing here cross-checks against the investor-presentation slide.
