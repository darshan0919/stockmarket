# Order book comparison — RVNL vs NCC vs KIRLOSENG

**Short answer: RVNL has the strongest revenue visibility on the raw arithmetic — ₹1,05,085 Cr of unexecuted work, 5.15x FY26 sales, versus NCC's ₹87,079 Cr at 4.18x. But it's the lower-quality 5.15x, and on the measure that decides whether visibility survives the next two years — replenishment — NCC is clearly ahead. Kirloskar Oil Engines doesn't belong in the comparison: it discloses no company-wide order book at all.**

## Why the headline ranking is misleading

Three things cut against RVNL's higher multiple.

**RVNL is not replacing the work it burns.** In FY26 it booked ₹5,875 Cr of new standalone orders against ₹20,412 Cr of revenue — it consumed roughly three and a half times more backlog than it added. NCC booked ₹31,884 Cr against ₹20,823 Cr, growing its book 16% year-on-year to a record. A 5.15x book that is shrinking converts into less future revenue than a 4.18x book that is growing. RVNL's post-concall pace has picked up sharply (₹5,823 Cr in four months, roughly triple the FY26 run rate), so this may be turning — but four months of back-ended, lumpy awards is not yet a trend.

**About 13% of RVNL's addition is tax, not work.** Five of its seven filings quote the contract value _including 18% GST_ and never state the ex-GST figure — including the largest, the ₹2,977 Cr NMDC Vizag award. Concall order books are reported ex-GST, so the like-for-like number is roughly **₹1,04,306 Cr (5.11x)**. Every NCC letter is explicitly "excl. GST" and needs no adjustment.

**RVNL's multiple is flattered by stalled execution.** Its sales have been flat around ₹20,000 Cr for three years, and Q4FY26 EBITDA margin fell to 5.83% from 10.36% the prior quarter. A book looks larger in years-of-revenue terms precisely when revenue isn't growing. NCC's book, by contrast, is 74% covered by price-escalation clauses, is 95%+ government/PSU counterparties, and management confirmed it is fully cleared for execution with no client-side delays.

The honest read: **RVNL if you want the biggest pile of committed work and a dated delivery calendar; NCC if you want visibility that is still being renewed and is better protected on price.**

NCC's intake is heavily back-ended (Q4FY26 alone was ₹9,573 Cr), so three months is weak evidence — but combined with management withdrawing FY27 revenue, margin and inflow guidance entirely, it is worth watching.

## KIRLOSENG — not applicable

Kirloskar Oil Engines does **not** disclose a company-wide order book, and this is a property of the business rather than a hole in the data. It sells engines, gensets and pumps on a short-cycle book-and-ship basis through a distribution channel; order book is an EPC/defence/capital-goods concept and doesn't describe how KOEL earns revenue.

The only backlog figure on its Q4FY26 concall is **₹798 Cr of "Order Book (Specialized)" — the combined NPCIL nuclear and Marine project backlog**, a product-line sub-figure representing about 0.10x of FY26 sales. Management noted the NPCIL portion runs to 2029 and that quarterly billing on it has not yet begun. Treating that ₹798 Cr as comparable to RVNL's ₹1,05,085 Cr would be a category error.

For KOEL, forward visibility lives in different metrics: 50,000+ powergen units sold (+41%, versus industry +18%), ₹1,400 Cr of FY27–FY28 capex targeting ₹5,000–6,000 Cr of peak incremental revenue, and data-centre and High-Horsepower penetration.

## Caveats

- Both running totals add **gross** order wins to a **net** concall base without subtracting revenue executed since Mar-26, so both read slightly high. The Q1FY27 concall will reset the base.
- RVNL's ex-GST figures are **derived** (÷1.18), not filed — the filings state only the gross amount.
- Base staleness is fine for both: Mar-26 concall with filings captured through 2026-07-30 (RVNL) and 2026-07-29 (NCC). NCC's July monthly letter had not been filed as of the watermark.
- Book-to-bill denominators are FY26 consolidated sales from Screener.in for all three, so the multiples are comparable. NCC's concall quotes ₹20,944 Cr consolidated versus Screener's ₹20,823 Cr; the difference doesn't move the ratio.
- Nothing is left unresolved — no filing for any of the three is sitting in the LLM-fallback queue.

## Data-quality fix made during this run

KIRLOSENG had been recorded with a **company-wide order book of ₹798 Cr**, which was wrong. The extractor strips parenthetical qualifiers before matching, so the label `Order Book (Specialized)` reduced to a bare "Order Book" and passed as a company total. Left alone, the daily sync would have kept publishing a segment backlog as if it were the whole company's.

Three changes, following the skill's learning loop:

1. Taught the pattern library `specialized`/`specialised` as segment keywords, so the classifier rejects that label. (The skill's `--learn-segment` CLI could not be used — its source anchor has drifted from the current patterns file, and it exits telling you to edit manually.)
2. Recorded the judgment that no company-wide total exists for KIRLOSENG 202603, permanently, so it is never re-asked.
3. Fixed two plumbing gaps this exposed in `getCompanyOrderBook.ensureBase()`: a resolved-but-null verdict was about to be written as a base, which would have published a ₹0 order book as fact; and the ledger was consulted before the verdict, so a superseded base whose quarter still matched would have been re-served forever. Added `orderBookLedger.clearBase()` to retract a base auditably (the retraction is appended to `history`; nothing is deleted).

KIRLOSENG now reports `noOrderBookDisclosed` and the sync classifies it as not-applicable rather than refreshing a bogus figure. All 4 jobs-runtime test suites (21 tests) pass. The 7 failing suites elsewhere in the repo are pre-existing `screener-web` React component tests, unrelated to these files.
