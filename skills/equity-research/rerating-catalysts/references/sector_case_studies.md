## 1. Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) Value-Chain Hierarchy

In EMS, top-line growth can be misleading if a company is confined to low-margin assembly. True re-rating requires climbing the value chain within existing and new customer accounts.

| Tier                                              | Activity                                                | Typical OPM   | Working Capital Days | Re-rating Potential                    |
| :------------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------ | :------------ | :------------------- | :------------------------------------- |
| **Tier 1: Box Building**                          | Low-complexity mechanical assembly                      | 2.5% – 3.5%   | 30–45 days           | Low; commodity pricing                 |
| **Tier 2: PCBA (Printed Circuit Board Assembly)** | Surface-mount technology (SMT) lines                    | 4.5% – 6.5%   | 60–75 days           | Moderate; sticky contracts             |
| **Tier 3: Backward Integrated Components**        | Manufacturing own wire harnesses, sheet metal, plastics | 7.0% – 9.0%   | 75–90 days           | High; captures component margins       |
| **Tier 4: ODM (Original Design Manufacturing)**   | Co-designing IP, firmware, and architecture with OEM    | 9.0% – 14.0%+ | 90–120 days          | Exceptional; pricing power + high ROIC |

**Analytical Tell:** When reading concalls and investor presentations, track the proportion of revenue originating from ODM / component manufacture versus pure box-build. A 200 bps OPM expansion in EMS is equivalent to massive PAT accretion.

## 2. Shipbuilding & Heavy Engineering (S-Curve Re-acceleration)

- **Catalyst Archetype:** Regulatory unlock + multi-year capacity absorption + operating leverage.
- **Mechanism:** Shipyards have enormous fixed costs (dry docks, gantry cranes, marine engineering payroll). When capacity utilization is 40–50%, margins are 4–6% with high volatility.
- **The Inflection:** When domestic policy mandates indigenous defense manufacturing and global commercial yards face backlogs through 2030, order books step from $2\times$ book-to-bill to $5-8\times$. As yard utilization crosses 80%, fixed costs are fully absorbed, resulting in OPM jumping from 6% to 14–18% and PAT surging $3-5\times$.
- **Risk Gate:** Steel commodity escalation clauses and delivery penalty clauses. Check contract terms in concalls.

## 3. Capacity Ramp Template: KRN Heat Exchanger Case Study

- **Catalyst Archetype:** Massive brownfield/greenfield capacity multiplier + global OEM qualification.
- **Mechanism:**
  1. _Investment Phase (Base Building):_ Company invests ₹150–200 Cr in a new plant. Capex appears as Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP). Depreciation and finance costs hit P&L while production is minimal. Market ignores the stock.
  2. _Inflection Phase (Train B $\to$ C):_ Plant achieves commercial commissioning. Global HVAC OEMs (Daikin, Carrier) complete lengthy 12–18 month audit and qualification cycles.
  3. _Acceleration Phase:_ Order inflow converts to 300% volume growth. High-margin exports rise from 20% to 50% of revenue. Operating leverage kicks in as plant hits 70%+ utilization.
- **Checklist:** Track CWIP to Gross Block conversion date, customer qualification audits, and export realization delta.

## 4. Data Center Super-Cycle & Technological Substitution

- **Catalyst Archetype:** Industry technological shift creating new demand bottlenecks.
- **Mechanism:**
  - AI compute clusters generate extreme heat densities and bandwidth bottlenecks.
  - _Optical Transceivers vs. Copper:_ Within high-density server racks, copper cable attenuation forces substitution into high-speed optical interconnects. Companies manufacturing optical transceivers and precision optical components experience sudden $3-5\times$ order surges.
  - _Power Infrastructure:_ Switchgear, bus ducts, dry-type transformers, and specialized cooling (liquid cooling/chillers) experience lead times stretching from 12 weeks to 50+ weeks.
- **Tell:** S-curve theme maturity check. Year 1–2 of a technological substitution super-cycle yields maximum multiple expansion.

## 5. Non-Core Divestment & "Switch" Case Studies

- **Catalyst Archetype:** Shedding loss-making drag to reveal high-ROCE core business.
- **Mechanism:**
  - A conglomerate or mid-cap runs two divisions: Division A (High margin 18%, ROCE 25%) and Division B (Low margin 3%, continuous cash drain). Blended margins look mediocre at 7% and trailing P/E is depressed at 12x.
  - Management announces divestment, shutdown, or demerger of Division B.
  - Suddenly, Division A stands alone. Reported ROCE jumps to 25%, OPM jumps to 18%, and the stock receives a double-engine re-rating (P/E expands from 12x to 25x while EPS rises due to eliminated losses).
- **Historical Cases:** Crompton Greaves consumer demerger, Lumax Auto Technologies non-core exits, Tube Investments acquisition & turnaround playbook.
